About Me

Hello, my name is Yuntae Ju. My research focuses on behavioral epidemiology, population health, and chronic disease prevention. I am particularly interested in understanding how behavioral, environmental, and biological factors influence whether individuals adopt and maintain physical activity and other healthy behaviors.

A central goal of my research is to identify why some individuals successfully initiate and sustain healthy behaviors while others face persistent personal, social, environmental, or biological barriers. I am interested not only in examining how health behaviors affect disease risk, but also in developing evidence that can help individuals and populations become more physically active and maintain healthier lifestyles.

My academic background began with quantitative analyses of human performance and physiological data. This experience gradually expanded into broader questions about health behavior, population health, and chronic disease epidemiology. My training in exercise physiology provides a biological foundation for understanding how behavioral changes may influence metabolic, cardiovascular, and functional health.

I primarily work with longitudinal cohort studies, national health surveys, population-based datasets, wearable data, and physiological measures. I use these data to identify heterogeneous behavioral patterns, characterize long-term health trajectories, and examine how individual characteristics, neighborhood conditions, social environments, and biological responses interact to influence health behavior.

To address these questions, I apply epidemiologic methods, statistical modeling, longitudinal data analysis, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. I use these methods not as ends in themselves, but as tools to identify modifiable determinants of behavior, recognize populations facing barriers to healthy lifestyles, and develop more effective and personalized strategies for health promotion.

My broader interests include digital health, wearable sensing, biomarkers, multi-omics, and bioinformatics. I am particularly interested in integrating behavioral and biomedical data to better understand why individuals respond differently to the same environments or interventions and to inform biologically grounded approaches to behavior change.

My long-term goal is to become an independent epidemiologist who integrates behavioral science, statistics, machine learning, and biomedical data to develop scalable and evidence-based strategies that help individuals and populations adopt and sustain healthier behaviors.

Representative Publications

Physical Activity Phenotypes Identified by Latent Class Analysis and Their Associations with Metabolic Syndrome Among Korean Adults

Ju, Y., Lee, J., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to BMC Public Health
This study applied latent class analysis to nationally representative Korean adult data from the Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey to identify multidimensional physical activity phenotypes. The findings demonstrate how person-centered epidemiologic approaches can characterize heterogeneity in health behaviors, identify population groups with different behavioral patterns, and inform targeted strategies for cardiometabolic disease prevention.

Artificial Intelligence-Driven Athlete Performance Passport for Enhancing Anti-Doping Strategies in Elite Male Weightlifting

Ju, Y., Lee, J., Kim, Y., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to npj Digital Medicine
This study developed an Athlete Performance Passport framework using longitudinal international weightlifting competition data. Individual performance trajectories and deviations from expected progression were analyzed using statistical modeling and machine learning to support scalable, explainable, and evidence-based anti-doping surveillance.

Performance Modeling for Men’s 100 m Freestyle: An Artificial Intelligence and Longitudinal Analysis Approach

Ju, Y., Oh, M., Lee, J., Kim, Y., Jang, S.J., Yoo, J., Park, K., Kim, Y., Ryoo, H., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to Scientific Reports
This study combined longitudinal mixed-effects modeling, predictive modeling, and explainable artificial intelligence to investigate individual performance trajectories in elite men’s 100 m freestyle. The work illustrates how longitudinal and computational approaches can identify heterogeneous developmental patterns and support evidence-based evaluation of human performance.

Software

YTWGBS

YTWGBS is a graphical user interface software for whole-genome bisulfite sequencing analysis. It is designed to make complex DNA methylation analysis workflows more accessible to biomedical researchers without extensive command-line programming experience.

The software supports window generation, methylation data preprocessing, methylKit-based differential methylation analysis, candidate region identification, genomic annotation, biological profiling, visualization, and export-ready result tables.
