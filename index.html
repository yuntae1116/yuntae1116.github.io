About Me

Hello, my name is Yuntae Ju. My research examines how health behaviors and the environments in which people live shape their ability to lead healthy lives. I am particularly interested in how physical activity, dietary behavior, and other everyday behaviors influence obesity, cardiometabolic health, chronic disease risk, and long-term health trajectories.

A central question guiding my work is why some individuals are able to initiate and sustain healthy behaviors while others face persistent barriers, even when they understand the importance of exercise and healthy eating. Rather than treating health behavior as an isolated personal choice, I study how neighborhood environments, weather and climate, transportation systems, food prices, social and economic conditions, and individual characteristics make healthy behavior easier or more difficult to adopt and maintain.

I also examine how behavioral patterns accumulate over time and contribute to differences in obesity, metabolic health, cardiovascular health, chronic disease risk, physical function, and healthy aging. Healthy aging is therefore one important long-term outcome within my broader interest in understanding how people can maintain health across adulthood and throughout the life course.

My academic training began with quantitative analyses of human performance and physiological data. This background in exercise physiology provides a biological foundation for interpreting how physical activity and other behaviors may affect metabolic, cardiovascular, and functional outcomes. It also helps me connect population-level epidemiologic findings with plausible physiological mechanisms.

I primarily work with longitudinal cohort studies, national health surveys, population-based datasets, wearable and sensor data, geospatial measures, electronic health records, and physiological indicators. I use these data to identify heterogeneous behavioral patterns, characterize health trajectories, and investigate why individuals respond differently to similar environments, policies, or interventions.

To address these questions, I apply epidemiologic methods, causal inference, longitudinal data analysis, statistical modeling, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. I view computational and quantitative methods as tools for answering substantive population health questions, identifying modifiable determinants of behavior, detecting populations facing disproportionate barriers, and understanding heterogeneity in behavioral and health outcomes.

My long-term goal is to become an independent epidemiologist who integrates behavioral, environmental, physiological, and computational perspectives to understand how people can live healthier lives. Ultimately, I aim to generate evidence that supports scalable, effective, and equitable strategies for promoting healthy behavior, preventing chronic disease, and maintaining health across the life course.

Research Questions

Why do some individuals walk, exercise, and eat healthfully while others face persistent barriers to these behaviors? How do neighborhood walkability, transportation systems, weather, food environments, and economic conditions shape physical activity, dietary behavior, obesity, and cardiometabolic health? Why do individuals exposed to similar conditions follow different behavioral and health trajectories? Can longitudinal, wearable, geospatial, and physiological data be combined with epidemiologic and machine learning methods to identify modifiable determinants and support healthier lives?

Current Projects

Physical activity phenotypes and metabolic syndrome in nationally representative data; behavioral and physiological discordance in rural adults; longitudinal health and performance trajectory modeling; AI-supported anti-doping surveillance; and the development of reproducible analytical software for biomedical research.

Representative Publications

Physical Activity Phenotypes Identified by Latent Class Analysis and Their Associations with Metabolic Syndrome Among Korean Adults

Ju, Y., Lee, J., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to BMC Public Health
This study applied latent class analysis to nationally representative data from Korean adults to identify multidimensional physical activity phenotypes and examine their associations with metabolic syndrome. The findings demonstrate how person-centered epidemiologic approaches can characterize heterogeneity in health behavior, identify population groups with distinct activity profiles, and inform targeted strategies for cardiometabolic disease prevention.

Artificial Intelligence-Driven Athlete Performance Passport for Enhancing Anti-Doping Strategies in Elite Male Weightlifting

Ju, Y., Lee, J., Kim, Y., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to npj Digital Medicine
This study developed an Athlete Performance Passport framework using longitudinal international weightlifting competition data. Individual performance trajectories and deviations from expected progression were analyzed using statistical modeling and machine learning to support scalable, explainable, and evidence-based anti-doping surveillance.

Performance Modeling for Men’s 100 m Freestyle: An Artificial Intelligence and Longitudinal Analysis Approach

Ju, Y., Oh, M., Lee, J., Kim, Y., Jang, S.J., Yoo, J., Park, K., Kim, Y., Ryoo, H., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to Scientific Reports
This study combined longitudinal mixed-effects modeling, predictive modeling, and explainable artificial intelligence to investigate individual performance trajectories in elite men’s 100 m freestyle. The work illustrates how longitudinal and computational approaches can identify heterogeneous developmental patterns and support evidence-based evaluation of human performance.

Software

YTWGBS

YTWGBS is a graphical user interface software for whole-genome bisulfite sequencing analysis. It is designed to make complex DNA methylation workflows more accessible to biomedical researchers without extensive command-line programming experience.

The software supports window generation, methylation data preprocessing, methylKit-based differential methylation analysis, candidate region identification, genomic annotation, biological profiling, visualization, and export-ready result tables.
