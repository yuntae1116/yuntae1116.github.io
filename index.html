About Me

Hello, my name is Yuntae Ju. My research focuses on behavioral and environmental epidemiology, population health, and chronic disease prevention. I am particularly interested in understanding how social, economic, environmental, and individual conditions shape physical activity, dietary behavior, and other everyday health behaviors.

A central question guiding my work is why some individuals are able to initiate and sustain healthy behaviors while others face persistent barriers, even when they understand the importance of exercise and healthy eating. I am interested in how neighborhood environments, weather and climate, transportation systems, food prices, social conditions, and personal characteristics influence whether people walk, exercise, eat healthfully, and maintain these behaviors over time.

I also examine how these behavioral patterns accumulate across the life course to influence obesity, cardiometabolic health, chronic disease risk, functional health, and healthy aging. Rather than treating health behavior as an isolated personal choice, I study it as the product of interacting environmental, social, economic, and biological contexts.

My academic training began with quantitative analyses of human performance and physiological data. This background gradually led me toward broader questions about population-level behavior and chronic disease. My training in exercise physiology provides a biological foundation for interpreting how changes in physical activity and other behaviors may affect metabolic, cardiovascular, and functional outcomes.

I primarily work with longitudinal cohort studies, national health surveys, population-based datasets, wearable and sensor data, geospatial measures, and physiological indicators. I use these data to identify heterogeneous behavioral patterns, characterize long-term health trajectories, and investigate why individuals respond differently to the same environments, policies, or interventions.

To address these questions, I apply epidemiologic methods, causal inference, statistical modeling, longitudinal data analysis, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. I use computational methods as tools for answering substantive public health questions, identifying modifiable determinants of behavior, detecting populations facing disproportionate barriers, and generating evidence for more effective and equitable health promotion strategies.

My long-term goal is to become an independent epidemiologist who studies how environmental and social contexts shape health behavior and how these behaviors influence chronic disease and aging. Ultimately, I aim to develop scalable, evidence-based strategies that make healthy behavior easier to adopt and sustain across diverse populations.

Research Questions

Why do some individuals walk more than others despite living in the same neighborhood? Which populations are most affected by extreme heat or rising food prices? How do walkability, transportation systems, and food environments shape physical activity, dietary behavior, obesity, and cardiometabolic health? Can longitudinal and wearable data be used to predict individual behavior change and identify people who face persistent barriers to healthy lifestyles?

Current Projects

Physical activity phenotypes and metabolic syndrome in nationally representative data; behavioral and physiological discordance in rural adults; longitudinal health and performance trajectory modeling; AI-supported anti-doping surveillance; and the development of reproducible analytical software for biomedical research.

Representative Publications

Physical Activity Phenotypes Identified by Latent Class Analysis and Their Associations with Metabolic Syndrome Among Korean Adults

Ju, Y., Lee, J., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to BMC Public Health
This study applied latent class analysis to nationally representative data from Korean adults to identify multidimensional physical activity phenotypes and examine their associations with metabolic syndrome. The findings demonstrate how person-centered epidemiologic approaches can characterize heterogeneity in health behavior, identify population groups with distinct activity profiles, and inform targeted strategies for cardiometabolic disease prevention.

Artificial Intelligence-Driven Athlete Performance Passport for Enhancing Anti-Doping Strategies in Elite Male Weightlifting

Ju, Y., Lee, J., Kim, Y., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to npj Digital Medicine
This study developed an Athlete Performance Passport framework using longitudinal international weightlifting competition data. Individual performance trajectories and deviations from expected progression were analyzed using statistical modeling and machine learning to support scalable, explainable, and evidence-based anti-doping surveillance.

Performance Modeling for Men’s 100 m Freestyle: An Artificial Intelligence and Longitudinal Analysis Approach

Ju, Y., Oh, M., Lee, J., Kim, Y., Jang, S.J., Yoo, J., Park, K., Kim, Y., Ryoo, H., Kim, D.-H., & Suh, S.-H.
Submitted to Scientific Reports
This study combined longitudinal mixed-effects modeling, predictive modeling, and explainable artificial intelligence to investigate individual performance trajectories in elite men’s 100 m freestyle. The work illustrates how longitudinal and computational approaches can identify heterogeneous developmental patterns and support evidence-based evaluation of human performance.

Software

YTWGBS

YTWGBS is a graphical user interface software for whole-genome bisulfite sequencing analysis. It is designed to make complex DNA methylation workflows more accessible to biomedical researchers without extensive command-line programming experience.

The software supports window generation, methylation data preprocessing, methylKit-based differential methylation analysis, candidate region identification, genomic annotation, biological profiling, visualization, and export-ready result tables.
